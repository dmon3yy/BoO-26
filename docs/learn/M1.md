# M1 — How this works

**TL;DR:** M1 is about *feel*: how a jump lands, where the camera looks, and what happens when you rest or die. Almost all of it is numbers in `content/movement.json` and text pictures in `content/rooms/`, so you can tune the game's hands and draw new rooms without touching code. Arcadia now has eight rooms, all roughed in (a "greybox"), and the validator proves none of them can trap you.

## The feel toolkit

Platformers feel good because of small cheats the player never notices. Each one is a number in `content/movement.json`, and `step_motion` in `game/actors/player/orpheus.gd` applies them.

| Cheat | Number | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| **Coyote time** | `coyote_time` (0.09 s) | You can still jump a few frames after running off a ledge. Without it, jumps at the very edge feel "eaten". |
| **Jump buffer** | `jump_buffer` (0.12 s) | Pressing jump just *before* landing still jumps on landing. |
| **Variable jump** | `jump_cut` (0.42) | Let go early and the rise is cut short: tap for a hop, hold for full height. |
| **Apex hang** (new) | `apex_window`, `apex_gravity_scale` | Near the top of a held jump, gravity softens to 55%. That moment of float is where you aim. |
| **Fast fall** | `fall_gravity` | Falling is heavier than rising, so jumps feel snappy instead of floaty. |
| **Turnaround** (new) | `turn_accel` | Reversing on the ground brakes twice as hard as starting, so changing direction feels planted. |
| **Corner nudge** (new) | `corner_nudge` (5 px) | Clip a ceiling corner by a few pixels and you slide around it instead of bonking your head. |
| **Landing dust** (new) | `hard_landing_speed` | A puff of snow when you land from a real fall: feedback that weight exists. |
| **Drop-through** | `DROP_TIME` in `orpheus.gd` (0.25 s) | Down + Jump on a thin ledge (`=`) drops you through it instead of jumping. On solid ground it's a normal jump. |

**Hitstop** (`game/systems/hitstop.gd`) freezes the game for a tenth of a second. It's used on death now and will make every club hit land in M2. Fighting games and *Hollow Knight* use the same trick.

**Why tests trust it:** `tests/unit/test_movement.gd` and `test_feel.gd` run `step_motion` frame by frame and check the claims: a full jump really clears 3 tiles up and 4 across, holding jump really hangs longer at the top, and reversing really brakes harder. The world validator uses the same limits, so if a test passes, "reachable" in the validator means reachable in your hands.

## The camera

The camera (in `game/world/world_manager.gd`) does three things:
- **Looks ahead** 28 px in the direction you face, so you see what you're running into.
- **Holds its height while you jump.** It only re-centres when you land, climb more than 56 px above where you took off, or drop below it. Without this the screen bobs on every hop, which is tiring to watch.
- **Glides between rooms** Super Metroid-style, and never shows past a room's edge.

## Hearths, death and the half-olive rule

- **Resting:** stand by a Hearth and press **Up**. Three things happen. It becomes your respawn point, the game saves to your browser, and Hestia shows your **Word**. The Word is your save written as a short code (about 13 characters this early), so copying it somewhere safe protects your progress even if browser storage gets wiped. Press Jump or Esc, or walk away, to close it.
- **How the Word stays short:** instead of spelling the save out, it *numbers* things. `content/word.json` gives every ability, flag and season a fixed position, so "has the Sandals" is a single bit. Hearths, rooms and keepsakes are numbered in `world.json` order. That's why both lists are **append-only**: reorder them and old Words read back wrong. Olives lying in rooms aren't in the Word (they reappear if you restore from one), but your olive count is.
- **Dying:** icicles (`^`), freezing water (`~`) and falling out of the world kill you. The game freezes for a beat (hitstop), fades out, and you wake at your last Hearth.
- **The half-olive rule** (your design change): you drop **half** your olives, rounded up, where you fell. Die again before you get back to them and that pile is gone. It's `drop_olives_on_death` in `game/autoload/game_state.gd`, and `tests/unit/test_save.gd` checks it (30 → 15 dropped, 7 → 4 dropped).
- **Continue:** reloading the page puts you back at your last Hearth.

## The auto-map

Press **Tab** or **M** (or the gamepad's Back button). The game pauses and shows every room you've visited on the world grid. The room you're in is lit, Orpheus is the blinking dot, and Hearths are orange embers. The map is drawn straight from `content/world.json`, so adding a room needs no extra map work.

## The greybox of Arcadia

"Greybox" means layout first, art later: rooms built from the existing tiles to test how they *play*. Arcadia is now eight rooms:

```
 cell:   0     1     2     3     4     5     6     7
 row 0  [Frozen Grove][Tmpl][Shep]            [Herm][Zeus]
 row 1                      [Drop][Frozen Brook][Climb]
 row 2                            [Olive Caves ][Bull]
```
Tmpl = Temple Steps, Shep/Drop = Shepherd's Drop (two cells tall), Herm/Climb = Hermit's Climb (two cells tall, Hearth at the bottom), Zeus = Zeus's Altar, Bull = the Bull's Pen.

The intended loop is Grove → Temple Steps → down Shepherd's Drop → across the Brook → (Hearth) → up Hermit's Climb → Zeus's Altar. The optional loop goes through the thin ice at the Brook's east end, into the Olive Caves (low ceilings: corner-nudge practice), through the Bull's Pen, and climbs back up to the Hearth.

The rooms are text files like every other room. The world validator checks openings line up, every olive is reachable, and no spot can trap you (`python3 tools/validate_world.py` → `world OK`).

## Try it yourself (5 minutes each)

1. **Make Orpheus floatier.** In `content/movement.json`, change `apex_gravity_scale` from `0.55` to `0.3`. Run the tests (see Commands). The feel test still passes; the jump just floats more.
2. **Break the validator on purpose.** In `content/rooms/arcadia_04.room`, widen a water pool from 3 to 5 `~`. Run the validator: it reports the far side as unreachable, because a jump only crosses 4 tiles.
3. **Add a Hearth.** Put an `H` on a floor tile in `arcadia_07.room` (on an empty `.` just above a `#`). Rest there in the game.
4. **Retune the camera.** In `world_manager.gd`, set `LOOK_AHEAD` to `60`. Notice how much more the view leads you, and decide whether you like it.

## Key takeaways
- Game feel is a stack of small, named cheats, and each one is a number you can tune.
- The camera's job is calm: lead the player, and don't bob.
- Hearths save, set the respawn point and hand you a portable Word; death costs half your olives.
- Rooms are text. The validator proves they're fair before you ever play them.
